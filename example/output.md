# Diagnosis: Three Stalled Approvals

Source: [cases.md](cases.md), fictional.

## Case A: Alex

**Diagnosis: Indecision.** The manager has approved similar arrangements before, so this is not a policy or precedent problem in principle. No specific concern has been raised across three weeks of deferral. This reads as a genuine fear of making the wrong call this particular time, not a stated objection or a dependency.

**What actually helps:** make the decision feel safer, not more urgent. A trial period with a set review date, framed as reversible rather than permanent, addresses the likely fear directly. Pushing for a faster answer does not address it.

## Case B: Priya

**Diagnosis: Approval dependency.** This is not indecision at all. A specific, named external process (HR's policy update) has been stated as the actual blocker, with a stated timeframe. Nothing about this delay depends on the manager's own comfort with the decision.

**What actually helps:** nothing to reassure here; the honest next step is following up with HR's timeline directly, not treating the manager as the person who needs convincing.

## Case C: Sam

**Diagnosis: Soft no.** Five deferrals over two months, slowing response times, and no specific concern named even when asked directly. This pattern, increasing avoidance without a stated reason, reads as a decision against that has not been said outright, not genuine indecision.

**What actually helps:** asking directly and plainly whether this is actually not going to happen, rather than continuing to defer to a sixth round. Treating this the same as Alex's case and offering more reassurance would miss what the pattern is actually showing.

## Summary

Three requests, three genuinely different situations: one needs the decision made to feel safer, one needs nothing but time for an external dependency to clear, and one needs a direct, honest question rather than another round of patience.
