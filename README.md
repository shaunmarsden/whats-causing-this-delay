Work out whether someone's delay on a decision is indecision, a concern they haven't voiced, a real wait on someone else's approval, or a soft no, before you decide how to respond.

## Why

"Let me think about it" can mean several different things, and each needs a different response. Pushing for a faster answer doesn't help with indecision, and does nothing when they're waiting on someone else. Treating a soft no as a need for more patience only puts off a straight answer.

[![Five possible causes of an ongoing delay.](assets/diagrams/01-whats-causing-this-delay.svg)](SKILL.md)

**Not what you need?** This is for a decision that's still open. If it has already closed, been turned down or gone quiet for good, [Post-Mortem Builder](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/post-mortem-builder) is probably the one you want.

## Use It

Copy [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) and paste it into your AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar). Then paste in the exchange so far and how long it's gone on. It diagnoses:

- **Indecision**, positive, no stated concern, a fear of making the wrong call, helped by making the decision feel safer, not by pushing
- **Unstated concern**, something specific they haven't yet said outright
- **Approval dependency**, a real, named process the decision depends on, where reassurance changes nothing
- **Soft no**, repeated putting off with no real movement, most likely a decision against
- **Disqualification**, the fit was never really there

<details>
<summary><strong>See exactly what it produces</strong></summary>

1. Which of the five states fits, with fact kept apart from inference
2. What would help in that state, not generic patience or pushing
3. A plain statement when the evidence points to a soft no or disqualification, not a hedge to stay hopeful
4. A refusal, not a draft, if asked for a concession or incentive to speed things up

</details>

See [the worked example](example/): three made-up employees each waiting on a manager's decision. One is indecision, one a real, named dependency, one a soft no. Each needs a different response, not the same generic patience. [The second worked example](example-two/) has the harder cases: a concern mentioned once then dropped, a real disqualification, and a request to draft an incentive.

Use [the blank template](templates/diagnosis-template.md) for your own case, and [the review checklist](checks/checklist.md) before you decide how to respond.

No installation, project or coding needed to try it once.

## Before You Use It

This only diagnoses. What to say or do next, and any message you send, is your call.
